How to Add a Contact Form to a Static Website (Formspree or Netlify Forms)

Published 2026-11-07 · Cometrix Web Studio guides

A static website (plain HTML, CSS and images) is fast, cheap to host and hard to break. Its one obvious gap is forms: there's no server-side code to receive a submission and email it to you. The fix is to hand that job to a form service. This guide shows the two most common approaches, Formspree and Netlify Forms, with working markup, spam protection and a testing checklist.

How static-site forms work

An HTML form sends its fields to whatever URL is in its action attribute. On a traditional site, that URL is a script on your own server. On a static site, you point it at a service that:

  1. receives the submission,
  2. filters out spam,
  3. stores it in a dashboard, and
  4. emails it to you.

You write normal HTML. The only differences are the action URL (for Formspree) or a special attribute (for Netlify).

Before you start: design a form people will finish

For a local service business, a contact form is a lead form. Every extra field costs you some people who give up. A good default:

  • Name
  • Phone (and/or email)
  • Town or ZIP code, so you can confirm it's in your area
  • What do you need help with? (a short text box)
  • Optional: preferred time to contact

Use the right input types (type="tel", type="email") so phones show the right keyboard, give every field a visible <label>, and mark only the truly necessary fields required. Above the form, add a line for urgent jobs: "Emergency? Call (555) 555-0123." Forms are for jobs that can wait an hour.

Option A: Formspree (works on any host)

Formspree works on GitHub Pages, Cloudflare Pages, Netlify or any other host, because the form posts straight to Formspree's servers.

Setup

  1. Create a Formspree account and verify your email.
  2. In the dashboard, create a new form. Formspree's HTML form guide explains that your form's unique endpoint appears in the Integration section.
  3. Put that endpoint in your form's action. Formspree's docs say a form needs, at minimum, the action attribute with your endpoint and inputs with a name attribute; any element with a name is sent.

Markup

<form action="https://formspree.io/f/YOUR_FORM_ID" method="POST">
  <label for="name">Name</label>
  <input id="name" name="name" type="text" autocomplete="name" required>

  <label for="phone">Phone</label>
  <input id="phone" name="phone" type="tel" autocomplete="tel" required>

  <label for="email">Email (optional)</label>
  <input id="email" name="email" type="email" autocomplete="email">

  <label for="zip">ZIP code</label>
  <input id="zip" name="zip" type="text" inputmode="numeric" autocomplete="postal-code">

  <label for="message">What do you need help with?</label>
  <textarea id="message" name="message" rows="4" required></textarea>

  <!-- Honeypot: hidden from people, tempting to bots -->
  <input type="text" name="_gotcha" style="display:none" tabindex="-1" autocomplete="off">

  <button type="submit">Send request</button>
</form>

The _gotcha field is Formspree's honeypot. Its honeypot documentation explains that a hidden input with that name catches automated scrapers that fill in every field.

Free plan limits

Formspree's plans page lists the Free plan with 50 submissions per month, a 30-day submission archive, two linked emails and basic spam filtering. A custom "thank you" page isn't included on Free. For a small business that gets a few inquiries a week, 50 a month may be enough; if you're near the limit, the paid tiers raise it.

Option B: Netlify Forms (if you host on Netlify)

If your site is hosted on Netlify, you can skip the third-party service. Netlify's forms setup documentation explains that its build system parses your HTML at deploy time and detects forms automatically, with no extra JavaScript or API calls.

Setup

  1. In the Netlify UI, go to Forms and select Enable form detection. Detection starts with your next deploy.
  2. Add data-netlify="true" (or a netlify attribute) and a unique name to your form tag.
  3. Redeploy. Submissions appear under Forms in your site dashboard, and you can set up email notifications there.

Markup

<form name="contact" method="POST" data-netlify="true" netlify-honeypot="bot-field">
  <p hidden>
    <label>Don't fill this out: <input name="bot-field"></label>
  </p>

  <label for="name">Name</label>
  <input id="name" name="name" type="text" required>

  <label for="phone">Phone</label>
  <input id="phone" name="phone" type="tel" required>

  <label for="message">What do you need help with?</label>
  <textarea id="message" name="message" rows="4" required></textarea>

  <button type="submit">Send request</button>
</form>

A few details from Netlify's docs worth knowing:

  • The form's name is what the form is called in the Netlify UI. If you have several forms, each needs a different name.
  • During deploy, Netlify strips the data-netlify attribute and injects a hidden input named form-name. If your form is rendered by JavaScript rather than present in the HTML, you need to include that hidden field yourself and make sure a static copy of the form exists for detection.
  • Per Netlify's spam filter docs, all submissions are filtered with Akismet, and you can add a honeypot field (as above, via netlify-honeypot) or a reCAPTCHA 2 challenge for extra protection.
  • The forms billing page says forms are free and unlimited on credit-based plans; older legacy plans are metered differently.

Showing a thank-you message

By default, both services show their own confirmation page after a submission. Options for something friendlier:

  • Netlify: add an action attribute pointing to your own page, such as action="/thanks/", and create that page.
  • Formspree: custom thank-you pages are a paid-plan feature according to its plans page, but Ajax forms are available on Free, so you can submit with JavaScript and show a message on the same page.

Whatever you show, tell the person what happens next: "Thanks, we'll call you back within two business hours. If water is actively leaking, call us at (555) 555-0123."

Testing checklist before you rely on it

  1. Submit the live form from your phone and from a computer.
  2. Confirm the email arrives, and check your spam folder. If it lands there, mark it "not spam" and add the sender to your contacts.
  3. Check that every field you care about shows up in the email. A missing name attribute is the most common reason a field doesn't arrive.
  4. Try submitting with a required field empty to make sure the browser stops it.
  5. Make sure the page is served over HTTPS. Chrome flags forms on insecure pages, and now warns before connecting to HTTP sites at all. See our guide to fixing 'Not secure'.
  6. Have someone outside your business test it once a month. Forms break silently when an email address changes or a free plan limit is hit.

Common problems and fixes

  • Submissions never arrive (Netlify). Check that form detection is enabled and that you redeployed after enabling it. Netlify finds forms by parsing your HTML at deploy time, so a form that only exists after JavaScript runs won't be detected unless a static copy is present.
  • Submissions never arrive (Formspree). Confirm you verified your email address and that the action URL contains your exact form ID. Look in the Formspree dashboard: if submissions appear there but not in your inbox, the problem is email filtering, not the form.
  • Some fields are missing. Every input needs a name attribute. An id alone isn't sent.
  • Lots of spam. Make sure the honeypot field is present and hidden, and consider adding reCAPTCHA (both services support it). Avoid publishing your raw email address next to the form; it attracts spam on its own.
  • It worked last month but not now. Check whether you've hit a free plan's monthly limit, or whether the notification email address was changed.

Privacy basics

You're collecting names and phone numbers, so add a short line near the form explaining how you use them ("We only use your details to respond to your request"), and link to a privacy page if your region or industry requires one. Don't ask for sensitive information you don't need. GitHub's own documentation, for instance, notes that Pages sites shouldn't be used for sensitive transactions like passwords or credit card numbers, and a contact form has no business collecting those anyway.

Summary

Adding a working contact form to a static site takes about 15 minutes: pick Formspree or Netlify Forms, add the endpoint or attribute to an ordinary HTML form, include a honeypot, and test the whole path from the form to your inbox. That's all the "backend" most local service businesses need.

How to Choose a Domain Name for Your Small Business (and Avoid the Mistakes)

Published 2026-11-07 · Cometrix Web Studio guides

Your domain name is your address on the internet, and for a local service business it is often the first thing a customer types after hearing your name. A good one is short, easy to say out loud, and hard to misspell. A bad one quietly costs you calls. This guide walks through how to pick one, where to register it, and the mistakes that are expensive to undo.

What a domain name does (and doesn't do)

A domain name points visitors to your website and gives you a matching email address. It is not a ranking factor on its own: stuffing keywords into a domain ("bestplumbercleveland.com") does not guarantee top Google results, and search engines treat a domain as one signal among many. What a domain does affect is whether people can find you by typing it, whether the link looks trustworthy, and whether your business email looks professional instead of like a personal one.

How to pick one people can type and remember

Use this shortlist as a filter. A good domain passes all of these:

  • Short. Aim for under 15 characters if you can. Every extra character is another chance to mistype it.
  • Sayable. Say it out loud to a friend and see if they can spell it back. If you have to spell it letter by letter, it is too complicated.
  • No hyphens or numbers. Hyphens are easy to forget, and numbers create ambiguity ("is that the number 5 or the word five?"). They are also associated with low-quality sites.
  • .com when possible. It is still the default people type and the most trusted. A local TLD (like .us or a city TLD) can work, but be aware many customers will type .com out of habit.
  • Not a trademark. Avoid names that contain another company's brand, especially a competitor's. Search the USPTO database (or your country's equivalent) before you commit.

If your exact business name is taken

This is normal for established businesses. Work through these options in order:

  1. Add your location. "Rivera Plumbing" becomes "RiveraPlumbingAustin.com". This is usually the best fix because it also helps local search.
  2. Add a trade word. "RiveraPlumbing.com" → "RiveraPlumbers.com" or "RiveraPlumbingCo.com". Keep it natural.
  3. Add a modest prefix or suffix. "GetRivera.com", "RiveraPlumbingGroup.com". Avoid anything that looks like a spam pattern.
  4. Consider a different TLD only as a last resort. A .net or .co version of your exact name is fine if you can also point the .com at your site, but do not build your brand on a confusing extension.

Never register a hyphenated or misspelled version of a competitor's name. That is a fast route to a legal complaint and a bad reputation.

Where to register, and what to watch for

Register through a reputable registrar rather than letting a web designer or marketing agency buy it "for you" — the account should be in your name, with your email, and you should be able to export or transfer it at any time.

Before you buy, check four things:

  • Renewal price, not just the first year. Many registrars advertise a low first-year price and charge much more to renew. Look at the renewal rate before you decide.
  • Auto-renew is on. A lapsed domain can be snapped up by someone else within days, and getting it back is expensive or impossible. Use a payment method you keep current.
  • Two-factor authentication is on. Domain hijacking is real, and your domain is tied to your email and your website.
  • Contact details are yours and current. The email on the registration needs to be one you check, because renewal notices and transfer approvals go there. Consider turning on WHOIS privacy, which many registrars include free.

Common mistakes

  • Letting a freelancer or agency own the domain account. If you part ways, you may not be able to get your own address back.
  • Using a free domain bundled with a website builder. Those usually cannot be moved to another host later without cost and hassle.
  • Registering several similar names but not pointing them all at the site, so typos go nowhere.
  • Forgetting the renewal email because it went to an old address. Set a calendar reminder as a backup to auto-renew.
  • Buying a name you cannot say clearly on the phone — the exact moment a customer asks "what's your website?" is the worst time to discover it.

Quick checklist

  • Under 15 characters, no hyphens, no numbers, easy to spell out loud.

  • Renewal price checked before buying.
  • Domain connects to the website and the matching email address.

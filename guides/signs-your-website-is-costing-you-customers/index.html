7 Signs Your Website Is Costing You Customers (and What to Do About Each)

Published 2026-11-09 · Cometrix Web Studio guides

A business website can look fine to its owner and still quietly lose customers. This guide lists the seven problems we see most often on real small business sites, why each one costs you enquiries, and what to do about it — without guessing at numbers or blaming the wrong thing.

1. The phone number is not visible or not tappable

Most visitors to a service business site want to do one thing: call. If the number is buried in a contact page, shown as an image rather than text, or wrapped in a clickable tel: link only on some pages, you lose the visitor who was ready to call.

The fix: put the phone number in the header of every page as plain text, wrapped in <a href="tel:+15551234567"> so a phone can dial it with one tap. Test it on an actual phone, not on a desktop preview.

2. The site is not usable on a phone

Open your site on a phone and try to read it at arm's length. Text that requires zooming, buttons smaller than a fingertip, a menu that opens sideways, a table that runs off the edge — each of these sends visitors away. More than half of local searches happen on phones, and visitors who cannot read your site do not send you an email saying why.

The fix: design the phone layout first, not as an afterthought. Use font sizes of at least 16px for body text, tap targets of at least 44 pixels, and a layout that flows in one column. The free mobile-ready checklist walks through the checks.

3. The pages do not say what you do or where you do it

A visitor from a search should be able to answer three questions within seconds: what this business does, where it works, and how to reach it. Sites that open with a rotating banner, a generic welcome, or a history of the company answer none of them.

The fix: lead with a plain sentence — "Family-run plumbing company covering Tucson and the surrounding areas — call us at (555) 123-4567." Then list the services in the words customers actually search for, and name the towns you serve.

4. Nothing on the page shows you are trustworthy

A stranger deciding whether to call you is also deciding whether you will show up, quote fairly and do good work. The signals they look for are simple: licenses and certifications, years in business, real reviews, photos of real work or the real team, a full address or service area, and a way to reach a person.

The fix: add what is true — never invented years, reviews or "certifications". Real photos of real jobs beat stock imagery. Get genuine reviews moving with an ethical review process and link to them.

5. The design looks out of date

Visitors read your site as a proxy for your business. A design that references a past decade — heavy gradients, tiny type, a Flash-style animation, or a layout that predates phones — suggests a business that may also be a decade behind on the actual work. That is not vanity: for bigger-ticket jobs, people pay a premium for evidence of competence.

The fix: this is a rebuild decision, and a template covers most of it. The Local Business Website Template Pack contains ten mobile-first templates with the structure above already in place.

6. The site is slow

Slow sites lose the visitor before the first screen finishes. On phones, a page that takes more than a few seconds to load loses a large share of visitors — many of whom never come back. Common causes are oversized images, a pile of old scripts and third-party widgets, and cheap shared hosting with a cached response times measured in hundreds of milliseconds.

The fix: run the speed checklist. Compress images to WebP, remove scripts nobody uses, and enable caching. Then measure with PageSpeed Insights before and after, so you know whether the change did anything.

7. Broken and missing basics

Check these on your own site right now — they are the most common silent killers:

  • Contact form that does not arrive. Submit it yourself and confirm the email lands.
  • Broken links and dead "Book now" buttons. A visitor who clicks a dead call to action does not try again.
  • Old copyright year and expired specials in the footer, which signal a site nobody maintains.
  • No HTTPS — the browser "Not secure" label does more damage than most owners expect. The fix is free; see the HTTPS guide.
  • No page title or meta description, so search results show a URL or a truncated mess. The free title and meta checker shows your search preview.

How to decide: fix, refresh, or rebuild

What you foundWhat to do
Missing phone link, wrong title, dead form, stale footerFix in place — hours, not days. Do this first regardless.
Structure and content problems, but the business information is rightRefresh: new layout over the same content, keep the domain.
Design, phone usability and speed all fail, or the site is on an unmaintained platformRebuild on a template. Same domain, same enquiries, a site you can keep current.

Start with the fix-in-place items: they are cheap, they are yours to do, and they stop the bleeding while you decide about the rest. Then judge the bigger decision on the same evidence — your own phone, your own eyes, and a stopwatch.

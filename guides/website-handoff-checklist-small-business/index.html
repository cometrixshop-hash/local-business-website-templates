Website handoff checklist for small business owners

Published 2026-11-09 · Cometrix Web Studio guides

Before you approve a new business website, ask the person who built it to show you how it works. A good-looking homepage doesn't tell you whether enquiries reach your inbox or whether you can renew the domain next year. Use this checklist at handoff, while fixes are still part of the conversation.

Start with accounts you control

Write down where the domain is registered, where the site is hosted, and which service handles contact forms. Check that the business owner has access to each account through an email address they control. A developer can have separate access without being the only person who can sign in.

Don't ask anyone to email passwords. Use the service's invitation or access-sharing process where available. Store recovery information securely, not in a public project folder. If an account belongs to the developer, agree in writing how it will be transferred and whether any fees apply.

Try the customer journey on your phone

Open the homepage on your own phone, rather than watching a desktop demonstration. Find the service you sell, check that the service area is clear, and follow the main enquiry button. Read the page without zooming. Look for text hidden behind banners and menus that don't close.

If a button opens the phone dialler, check the number before making a test call. If it opens an email app, check the destination address. Test any booking link too, but don't create a paid booking or charge a card just to prove that a button works.

Confirm that a form reaches a person

Arrange a test with whoever receives enquiries. Submit a clearly labelled test message using an address you control. Confirm receipt in the destination inbox, including the message and reply address. A success message on the website alone isn't proof of delivery.

Try the form with a required field empty. Check that the error tells you what to fix and that you can reach the fields and submit button with the keyboard. Ask who monitors failed deliveries and whether the form service has usage limits. Delete the test enquiry afterward if it isn't needed.

Read the business details out loud

Review opening hours, service descriptions, prices, and geographical coverage with the owner. Check that photos belong to the business or are licensed for this use. Remove placeholder testimonials and any qualification the owner can't substantiate.

Make a separate list of statements that need routine updates, such as seasonal opening hours or starting prices. Assign someone to maintain that list. Don't leave a made-up launch date or sample address on the contact page because it looks plausible.

Get the files and the running costs

Ask for the agreed source files, images, and a backup or export appropriate to the platform. Some hosted builders don't export a complete working site; establish that limitation before assuming you can move it elsewhere. For a custom site, ask how a replacement developer would build and publish it.

Record each recurring bill, its owner, renewal date, and what happens if it isn't paid. Include the domain, hosting, forms, booking software, and any paid extensions. Keep the current bill or plan details with the handoff notes rather than relying on a verbal promise that everything is free.

Agree on fixes before signing off

Separate defects from new requests. A form that sends to the wrong inbox is different from adding a second booking system. Put outstanding fixes in one shared list with the affected page, what you expected, and what happened. Screenshots help, but don't include customer information.

Ask for written confirmation of the support period, what it covers, and the cost of later changes. Don't assume that paying for the build includes unlimited maintenance. Finish with a short handoff document containing account owners, renewal dates, test results, backup instructions, and unresolved issues.

This is a practical acceptance checklist, not a security audit or a legal compliance assessment. It won't prove search rankings or future sales. It does give you a record of what you tested before taking responsibility for the site.

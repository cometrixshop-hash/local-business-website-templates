How to Set Up a Google Business Profile (Step-by-Step for Local Businesses)

Published 2026-11-08 · Cometrix Web Studio guides

A Google Business Profile is the listing that shows up when someone searches for your business or your service on Google Search and Maps. For most local service businesses it is the most valuable free marketing asset they have. This walkthrough takes you through setting one up properly, step by step, without the jargon.

First, check whether a listing already exists

Many businesses already have a profile they do not know about: a customer, a map user, or Google itself may have created one from public information. Before you create anything, search for your business at google.com/business and on Google Maps.

  • If a listing exists: choose "Claim this business" (or "Own this business?"). You are asking to become the verified owner of an existing listing.
  • If nothing exists: choose "Create a business" / "Add your business to Google".

Claiming an existing listing is usually better than creating a new one, because the existing listing may already carry reviews and history.

Step 1: Add the basics

Enter your business name, category, and whether customers visit you or you travel to them. Be exact: the name should match your shop sign and every other place it appears online.

Step 2: Verify the business

Google wants to confirm the business is real before it shows your edits. Verification options vary by business type and country, and can include a postcard to your address, a phone call or text, an email, or a short video showing your location and equipment. Some businesses can verify instantly.

While you wait, you can usually still enter most of your information. It simply will not go live until verification completes.

Do not use a made-up address or a virtual office you do not actually work from. Google can and does remove listings for address violations, and a removed listing takes your reviews with it.

Step 3: Get the name, address and phone number exactly right

These three details are often called "NAP". They should be identical everywhere your business appears: your profile, your website, your social pages, and every directory. Even small differences (St. versus Street, a missing suite number, a tracking phone number) can confuse both customers and Google.

Use the phone number customers should call, not a call-tracking number that changes depending on where the click came from.

Step 4: Choose categories and services

Your primary category is the single most important one, because it strongly influences which searches your business can appear in. Pick the category that best describes your main service, then add secondary categories for the rest.

Within each category you can usually list specific services. Add the services customers actually search for, and skip the ones you do not offer. A shorter, accurate list beats a long padded one.

Step 5: Set your service area

How you handle location depends on how you work:

  • Storefront — customers visit you (a salon, a shop, a clinic). Show your address and a map pin.
  • Service-area business — you travel to customers (a plumber, a cleaner, a roofer). You can hide your exact address and instead list the cities or postcodes you serve.
  • Hybrid — you have a location and you also travel. Show the address and add the areas you cover.

Be honest about where you actually work. Listing a city you rarely serve leads to bad calls and unhappy customers.

Step 6: Add hours

Set your regular hours, and keep them updated. Add special hours for holidays, and use the "more hours" options (for example, a senior shopping hour) only if they really apply. If you close unexpectedly, mark the business temporarily closed rather than leaving stale hours up.

Step 7: Write the business description

You get up to 750 characters. Use them to say plainly what you do, where you work, and what makes you different. Write it for a customer, not for a search engine: what you offer, the area you cover, and how to get in touch. Do not stuff it with keywords or promotional claims.

Step 8: Add photos and videos

Photos are the easiest win in the whole profile. Upload a clear exterior shot (so people can find you), interior shots, pictures of your team at work, and examples of finished jobs. Keep them current and real, and avoid heavy logos or text overlays. Videos are optional but can help, as long as they genuinely show the business.

Step 9: Turn on the features that matter

  • Products and services — list what you sell, with prices where you can.
  • Posts — short updates about offers, events or news. They expire, so they only help if you keep posting.
  • Q&A — answer common questions, and keep an eye on questions other people add.
  • Messaging — optional; only enable it if you will actually reply.

Step 10: Start collecting reviews

Ask happy customers to leave a review, and make it easy by sharing your review link (find it in the "Ask for reviews" section of the profile). Never buy reviews, never offer payment or discounts for them, and never ask a customer to change a bad review in exchange for something. Google prohibits all of that, and penalties can remove your profile.

Keep it current

A profile is not a one-time task. Put a recurring reminder in your calendar to:

  1. Check for edits suggested by Google or the public, and accept or reject them.
  2. Reply to new reviews.
  3. Add a few new photos of recent work.
  4. Update hours for holidays and seasonal changes.
  5. Confirm your categories and services still match what you do.

Common mistakes

  • Creating a second listing when one already exists.
  • Keyword-stuffing the business name ("Bob's Plumbing | Best Plumber in Austin").
  • Listing a service area you do not actually cover.
  • Using a PO box or virtual office as a service-area address.
  • Setting messaging or posts live and then ignoring them.
  • Letting hours and holiday closures go stale.

Frequently asked questions

How does this tool work?

It queries crt.sh, a public search over Certificate Transparency logs, for certificates issued for your domain, picks the one with the latest expiry date and counts the days from today. All of the maths happens in your browser.

What do the colours mean?

Green: more than 30 days left. Amber: 30 days or fewer — renew now. Red: 14 days or fewer, or already expired — treat as urgent. If you use Let’s Encrypt, certificates are valid for 90 days and renew automatically, so a low number is normal as long as renewal is working.

Why does it say “no certificate found”?

Certificate Transparency logs can lag by hours, and a brand-new or private certificate may not be logged yet. Retry in a few minutes, and check the exact hostname (with or without www). Your browser’s padlock is the final authority on what is actually served.

Is an expired certificate really that bad for a small business?

Yes. Chrome and Safari show a full-screen warning and many visitors leave immediately — on mobile, most do. If your site is HTTP-only or the certificate has lapsed, you are paying for ads and visits that never become calls.

How often should I check?

If your hosting auto-renews (GitHub Pages, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, most managed hosts do), check once every few months or after any hosting change. If you install certificates by hand, check weekly.
